### Understanding Hair Fall During Washing

Experiencing significant hair fall every time you wash your hair can be concerning. While some hair shedding is normal (the average person loses **50–100 hairs per day**), excessive shedding during washing may indicate underlying issues such as:

- **Scalp conditions** (dandruff, dryness, or eczema)
- **Haircare practices** (aggressive brushing, heat styling, or harsh shampoos)
- **Nutritional deficiencies** (iron, vitamin D, protein)
- **Stress or hormonal changes**
- **Genetic factors** (e.g., androgenic alopecia)

Before switching shampoos, it’s a good idea to:
1. **Consult a dermatologist or trichologist** to rule out medical causes.
2. **Evaluate your overall haircare routine** (e.g., water temperature, brushing technique, frequency of washing).
3. **Check your diet and stress levels**.

## 🛁 **Additional Tips to Reduce Hair Fall While Washing**

1. **Use Cool or Warm (Not Hot) Water**  
   Hot water can shock hair follicles and increase breakage.

2. **Gentle Massaging**  
   Use your fingertips (not nails) to gently massage the scalp in circular motions.

3. **Limit Brushing**  
   Avoid brushing wet hair — it’s at its weakest. If you must, use a wide-tooth comb.

4. **Pat Dry, Don’t Rub**  
   Rubbing with a towel can cause friction and breakage. Use a soft microfiber towel or an old cotton T-shirt.

5. **Don’t Over-Wash**  
   Washing every 1–2 days is often enough. Over-washing strips natural oils that protect hair.

6. **Apply a Scalp Oil or Serum**  
   A few drops of **rosemary or peppermint oil diluted in a carrier oil** can stimulate growth. Apply pre-wash or post-wash.

Some hair loss in the shower is normal. Most people shed 50-100 hairs a day, and much of that comes out during washing, especially if you wash every few days, since the hairs that have already detached pile up and come out together.

**Signs it's probably normal**
- The hair has a small white bulb at the root (that's a natural shed)
- Your part and hairline look about the same as before
- The amount is fairly consistent

**Signs worth looking into**
- Noticeable thinning, a widening part, or a receding hairline
- A sudden increase in shedding (often follows illness, stress, surgery, childbirth, crash dieting, or starting or stopping medication, usually 2-3 months later)
- Broken, short hairs rather than full-length ones
- Itchy, flaky, or painful scalp

**Shampoo**
Shampoo doesn't cause or cure most hair loss, so switching brands rarely fixes it. Still, a few things can help:
- **Ketoconazole 1-2% shampoo** (e.g., Nizoral): if you have dandruff or an irritated scalp, it can help, and there's some evidence it may support hair density.
- **A gentle, sulfate-free shampoo**: helpful if your scalp is dry or irritated.
- **Conditioner**, applied to the lengths, reduces breakage when detangling.
- Avoid very hot water, harsh scrubbing, and brushing hard when wet.

Products marketed as "anti-hair-fall" shampoos mostly just clean and condition. Minoxidil is the over-the-counter ingredient with solid evidence for pattern thinning, but it's a topical treatment, not a shampoo.

**Common underlying causes**
Low iron or ferritin, thyroid problems, vitamin D deficiency, hormonal changes, genetics (pattern hair loss), and stress. These are worth checking if shedding is heavy or lasts more than 2-3 months.

If it's persistent or you're noticing thinning, a GP or dermatologist can run simple blood tests and examine your scalp. Early treatment works best for pattern hair loss.
